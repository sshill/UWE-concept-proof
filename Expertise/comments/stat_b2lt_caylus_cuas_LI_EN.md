Tim, your conclusion cuts straight to the core: a counter-drone defence is not the sum of its boxes, but what happens between them.

The French Army STAT and B2LT exercise at Caylus exposed the real-world operational bottleneck. Testing 83 systems from 34 vendors against unscripted, military-piloted threats across open, urban, and live-fire terrain strips away vendor marketing. Yet having 83 disparate boxes without an overarching architecture simply creates acoustic, RF, and cognitive saturation.

This is precisely why we architected the Unmanned Warfare Engine (UWE). UWE is not another bespoke effector or sensor. It is the Hardware Abstraction Layer (HAL) and deterministic middleware that unifies heterogeneous platforms into a single operational kill-web.

In UWE domain nomenclature, whether a sensor is radar, RF, or acoustic, it is abstracted into standardized Target Track Telemetry (TTT). Whether an effector is an EW jammer, kinetic munition, or interceptor drone, it exposes a uniform Execution API.

Testing at Caylus confirms that military acceptance cannot be about certifying isolated boxes. It requires standardizing the machine-speed arbitration between them.
