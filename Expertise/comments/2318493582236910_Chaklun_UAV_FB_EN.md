UWE Cyber-Physical Teardown: Chaklun Tactical UAV (Defense Advancement / Ukraine Defense Portal)

1. Engineering Deconstruction of the Tactical Airframe
The footage featured by Defense Advancement showcases the Ukrainian tactical fixed-wing UAV Chaklun, developed in Dnipro by the Aviation Federation of Ukraine. In sharp contrast to consumer quadcopters constrained by narrow battery radius, Chaklun is engineered on a classic high-wing pusher architecture with a wingspan of approximately 1.4 meters and an MTOW of 2.2-3.0 kg. The system complies strictly with the C0-C1 backpack containerization tier: it launches by hand without cumbersome pneumatic catapults and lands directly on its belly, enabling frontline crews to deploy from unprepared tree lines and dispersed infantry positions.

2. Aerodynamic Efficiency, Radio-Transparency, and EW Resilience
The airframe is molded from expanded polypropylene (EPP) reinforced with carbon-fiber spars. The absence of metallic skin delivers an ultra-low Radar Cross Section (RCS), making tracking by tactical air defense radars extraordinarily difficult. The high aerodynamic glide ratio of the fixed wing (L/D around 10-12) enables the electric powertrain to loiter for 60-120 minutes and cover routes exceeding 100 km at cruising speeds of 70-80 km/h. Crucially, Chaklun is designed for complete radio silence: it penetrates dense EW sectors autonomously via dead reckoning and Pitot-static airspeed sensing, recording high-resolution geo-tagged reconnaissance imagery onto onboard storage without transmitting revealing RF telemetry.

3. UWE Architecture and HAL Interface Contracts
Within the Unmanned Warfare Engine taxonomy, the system is codified as UAV: AH-EB-AR (Air, Hand-launched backpack, Electric Battery, Autonomous Resilient). The cyber-physical layer requires two fundamental HAL contracts: IRadioSilentDeadReckoningNav to execute precise waypoint routing and return-to-base maneuvers based on inertial dead reckoning when GNSS is completely denied, and IEppAeroelasticBellyLandingDrive to command flare angle-of-attack and motor cut-off during unpaved belly landings, safely dissipating kinetic energy through the sacrificial EPP foam structure.

4. Conclusion: Asymmetric Attrition and High-Volume Fixed Wings
Chaklun demonstrates the power of industrial pragmatism in modern warfare. Rather than relying on cost-prohibitive composite airframes that require factory repairs, combining expendable EPP foam with radio silence and autonomous dead reckoning creates a high-survivability tactical scout at scale. It represents a textbook case of rational engineering where every structural design choice maximizes combat survivability and rapid field turnaround directly within forward infantry units.
