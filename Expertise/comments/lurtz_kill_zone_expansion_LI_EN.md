Patrick Lurtz highlights the brutal physics of the expanding kill zone, but Western procurement still risks falling into the "Logistics Trap".

Treating autonomy merely as an uncrewed pack mule (CASEVAC, ammo resupply) while adversaries deploy distributed kinetic strike networks is an operational dead end. Autonomy must kill, breach, and loiter in high-threat sectors where manned hulls cannot survive.

Three engineering realities define this transition:

1. Decoupled Lethality:
A 20-50 km kill zone cannot be contested with bespoke $5M platforms. It requires an open Hardware Abstraction Layer (HAL) decoupling attritable chassis from modular payloads. If an uncrewed node cannot execute an autonomous sacrificial breach (OWM) at 15G or drop into a micro-amp sleep state (the "Zhdun" picket), it is merely an expensive target.

2. Breaking the 1-to-1 Teleoperation Bottleneck:
Under heavy EW, manual joystick control collapses. Autonomy is not about replacing the commander; it is about cognitive filtering: APM (300-400 motor adjustments) handled by onboard edge-AI, while the human retains monopoly on intent with low CPM (2-3 lethal sanctions).

Autonomy is an architecture, not a procurement line item.
