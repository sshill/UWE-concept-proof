Rishabh, your operational account from Kashmir cuts straight to the brutal physics of mass attrition: when 30 to 40 drones arrive in waves every 10 to 15 minutes, the traditional ground-based C-UAS paradigm collapses.

Ground radars face an insurmountable physics wall. Beam broadening at standoff ranges and severe terrain clutter turn an incoming swarm into a single unresolved blob. This forces tactical units to rely on vertical racers with heavy onboard optical cameras. But optical terminal homing is strictly a 1-to-1 engagement. In a swarm of 60 drones, an optical racer manually or semi-autonomously engages the first target it sees, leaving the other 59 to penetrate the line.

To break mass with mass, radar must move into the air. In the Unmanned Warfare Engine (UWE) taxonomy, this is codified under the UAV: AM-TD-RA class: a long-endurance MALE airborne sentinel.

Operating at 20,000 ft with look-down AESA radar and Space-Time Adaptive Processing (STAP), an airborne sentinel disaggregates the swarm blob into 60 individual 3D tracks and provides midcourse vectoring to distributed interceptor swarms, allowing them to engage 60 targets simultaneously.
