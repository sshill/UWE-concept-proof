⚡ HYDRODYNAMIC SURVIVABILITY: HOW A 4-FOOT HULL DEFEATS 30-FOOT WAVES

During its 127-day crossing, the tiny C-Star survived Tropical Storm Dolly and 30-foot (9-meter) cresting swells. Its resilience stems directly from scaling physics:

1. Zero Bending Moments (L³ Scaling Law):
Large naval hulls break apart when suspended across crests due to massive structural bending moments. At 1.3 meters in length, the C-Star behaves as a buoyant particle, floating with wave contours like a cork and evading destructive impact loads.

2. True Perpetual Endurance (Decoupled Energy Harvesting):
By drawing kinetic propulsion purely from aerodynamic wind forces and electrical avionics power from solar arrays, the vehicle achieves indefinite operational loiter at sea, closing satellite coverage gaps at near-zero operating cost.

3. Autonomous Polar Navigation:
The onboard STM32 computer autonomously processes wind polars in real time, calculating optimal tacking and jibing maneuvers to navigate against headwinds and hold virtual oceanic picket stations.
