1. The Historical Doctrine: Conceptual Precedent & Lineage

Zaporozhian Chaika (Lapstrake Clinker) & The Monitor Warning (1862)

16th-17th century Cossack naval fleets operated clinker-built 'Chaikas' (60 ft, 50-70 crew) constructed with overlapping oak and linden planks. Chaikas featured bundles of reeds strapped along the gunwales ('remi') providing reserve buoyancy, roll dampening, and unsinkability in heavy Black Sea waves while maintaining a razor-thin visual freeboard that evaded Ottoman galleys. In 1862, the Battle of Hampton Roads proved that small, low-freeboard armored craft render high-sided wooden fleet flagships obsolete overnight.

2. The Bureaucratic Trap & Empirical De-Noising

The British Admiralty Rating Trap & Speedboat Conversion Myth

Traditional navies categorize surface ships by displacement tonnage, armor thickness, and gun caliber—a colonial fleet dogma that paralyzed the Russian Black Sea Fleet. Conversely, converting civilian fiberglass planing speedboats into military drones fails in open seas: high-speed planing into Sea State 4+ generates extreme vertical accelerations (G_z > 6-8g), snapping composite stringers and destroying optical gimbal gyros. UWE mandates slender, wave-piercing displacement or semi-submersible hulls.
